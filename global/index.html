Discussions about Nvidia

Nvidia (NVDA) — the most-traded US stock on Tuesday 24 November 2026, closed at $155.38, down $9.67 (−5.86%), on about 379 million shares, the day after Monday's 31.48% rebound to $165.05.

Listen on Spotify · NVDA $155.38 · ~379M shares · ~$59B dollar volume · Day range $150.50–$159.92

Maya — the opportunity case

Fundamentals and opportunity

Maya argues the pullback is a healthy breather after a 31% one-day spike, not the start of another collapse. Even after Tuesday's fall, Nvidia is still up about 24% from Friday's close of $125.53, and the business hasn't changed since Monday: record revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103%, earnings per share of $2.68, and guidance of around $133 billion for the current quarter.

The customers still need the chips, the numbers are still extraordinary, and at $155 the stock is still a long way below its October high of $243.37. Monday proved the stock can move 30% on a headline; Tuesday proved it can give back 6% just as fast — that kind of volatility isn't a buying signal by itself, it's a warning to be patient.

Leo — the risk case

Risk and trust

Leo argues Tuesday was the hangover after Monday's stampede: a 31% day doesn't happen because investors calmly re-rated a company — it happened because the CEO came back, the shorts got squeezed, and the whole market bounced at once. On Tuesday the squeeze was over and buyers had to decide whether they actually wanted to own the stock at $165.

The sell-off was chip-wide (Micron down more than 10%, AMD about 9%, Broadcom 3%), Anthropic fell below its IPO price, and good results were sold anyway (Analog Devices −17.4%, HPE −16.3%), which says the doubts are about the whole AI trade. The SEC probe into Nvidia's accounting is still open and Jensen Huang is still summoned before House committees on 1 December.

Where Maya and Leo land

Monday was relief plus a squeeze; Tuesday was the market asking for proof. In the near term, expect more volatility until the PCE inflation data on Wednesday and the 1 December hearing. Longer term, the demand story is intact, but the stock has to earn the re-rating. What to watch: PCE inflation data on Wednesday, Dell and HP reporting after the close, markets closed Thursday for Thanksgiving with an early close Friday, then 1 December.

Reliance Worldwide (RWC) — the steepest climber in the ASX 200 on Tuesday 24 November 2026, up 11.92% to $5.07 with $278.1 million of value traded, after AustralianSuper said it would vote in favour of Brookfield's takeover.

Listen on Spotify · RWC $5.07 · $278.1M value traded · AustralianSuper holds 18.49%

Maya — the opportunity case

Fundamentals and opportunity

Maya argues Tuesday was the day Brookfield's takeover of Reliance Worldwide stopped being just a proposal and started looking like a path. AustralianSuper holds 18.49% of the company, and when the largest shareholder says publicly that it will vote in favour, the single biggest question hanging over the deal — will the register support it? — gets much closer to answered.

The market's verdict was emphatic: the stock jumped 11.92% to $5.07, the steepest climb in the whole ASX 200, and $278.1 million of stock changed hands, making RWC the fourth most valuable line of trade on the ASX that day.

Leo — the risk case

Risk and trust

Leo argues a takeover bounce is a one-day re-rating, not a business improvement. Reliance Worldwide didn't sell a single extra fitting on Tuesday; what changed was the perceived certainty of Brookfield's takeover. A share price that leaps almost 12% on a voting intention is a price that now assumes the process runs smoothly — and a stated intention to vote is not the same thing as votes in the ballot box.

He also points to the fuel around the fire: the ASX 200 rose 1.82% on Tuesday, its best day since 5 November, as an eight-session losing streak snapped. In a market bouncing that hard, the stock with genuine news was always going to run furthest — and if the broader market wobbles again, some of Tuesday's enthusiasm can unwind just as quickly.

Where Maya and Leo land

The trigger was AustralianSuper's backing: an 18.49% holder committing its vote turned Brookfield's takeover into the market's base case, and it is why RWC topped the ASX 200 on Tuesday. The fuel was the broad rebound — the ASX 200's biggest rise since 5 November after eight straight down sessions. What to watch next: the Brookfield vote and takeover process, the ABS October CPI print, and the US PCE inflation data.

Discussions about TSMC

TSMC 台積電 (2330) — the most-traded TAIEX stock by value on Tuesday 24 November 2026, closed at its 10% daily limit of NT$2,230, up 9.85%, contributing about 1,600 points to the TAIEX's record 3,310.46-point gain, after Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO. Discussion in Traditional Chinese — Mei leads, with Ming alongside.

Listen on Spotify · Discussion in Traditional Chinese · Mei & Ming · 6 min 37 sec · TSMC NT$2,230 · NT$29.47B value traded

Mei — the opportunity case

Fundamentals and opportunity

Mei argues Tuesday's limit-up was the market re-pricing TSMC, not a fluke. The trigger was Nvidia: the board reinstated Jensen Huang as president and CEO on Sunday, Nvidia jumped 31.48% in the US on Monday and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index rose 14.03%, and on Tuesday the whole Asian chip complex followed. TSMC had been sold down hard — from a NT$2,590 high on 6 October to a NT$2,000 close on 20 November, more than 20% in five weeks — so the rebound had oversold fuel behind it.

She reads the year's lowest turnover (NT$419.41 billion) as holders refusing to sell rather than buyers staying away: at the close, more than 21,000 buy orders were still queued unfilled. The breadth backs her up — 1,087 stocks rose against 31 falls, MediaTek, UMC and ASE all closed limit-up, the Taiwan 50 rose 9.59%, and the exchange cut the margin maintenance ratio from 130% to 120%, defusing forced selling. The fundamentals never changed: advanced-process orders and AI demand are intact. Faced with the limit-up, she wouldn't sell — sell at the limit and you can't buy it back.

Ming — the risk case

Risk and trust

Ming argues a record day built on the year's thinnest turnover is sellers stepping aside, not buyers charging in — and the biggest money was still heading the other way. Foreign investors net sold NT$50.39 billion on Tuesday, their seventh straight day of selling, even as the index posted its record gain. Overnight the US leg gave it back: Nvidia fell 5.86% and TSMC's ADR dropped about 4%, so where the rally started, it lasted one day.

He also flags the concentration: TSMC alone supplied about half the index's 3,310 points, so if the Nvidia story wobbles, the index has no cushion — and the exchange itself put five hot stocks under trading restrictions from 25 November. At NT$2,230 the stock is still more than 10% below its NT$2,590 October high, and Wednesday's open immediately tested the move, with the TAIEX down more than 3,400 points intraday and TSMC at NT$2,020. His question for anyone chasing: are you buying TSMC's future, or yesterday's limit-up? He would sell half into the limit-up and bank the profit.

Where Mei and Ming land

Nvidia restoring Jensen Huang was the trigger; the oversold bounce and reluctant sellers were the fuel. But the rebound is real yet unproven: it holds only if foreign investors stop selling — seven straight days and counting — and Nvidia's gains stick in the US. TSMC is still TSMC; the market's mood has come back, but the money hasn't. What to watch: the direction of foreign flows, and whether Nvidia holds its gains.

Mei: 所以我們的共識是：輝達換回黃仁勳是導火線，跌深和惜售是燃料，但是漲勢能不能延續，要看兩件事。第一，外資什麼時候停止賣超。第二，輝達的利多能不能持續發酵。台積電值不值得追，答案不在昨天的漲停，而在接下來這兩個問題的答案裡。
