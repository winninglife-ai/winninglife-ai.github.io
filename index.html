    Discussions about Nvidia

    One-off trial · Nvidia (NVDA) — the most-traded US stock on Monday 23 November 2026, closed at $165.05, up $39.52 (+31.48%), after Jensen Huang was reinstated as CEO. Maya and Leo work through why the volume exploded, from opposite sides.

    Maya — the opportunity case

    Fundamentals and opportunity

    “The business never actually changed. Those earnings — record revenue, 115.8 billion dollars, up 103 percent, guidance of 130 billion — none of that went away. At Friday’s close, Nvidia was trading at about 13 times forward earnings, against a five-year average of about 41. Even now, after the jump, it’s only about 17 times. The market wasn’t pricing the business on Friday, it was pricing the fear. And the fear was about one man leaving. Now he’s back.”

    “And I don’t think the board vote alone explains 776 million shares. That’s short covering, Leo. That’s a squeeze. ORTEX estimated short sellers lost about 12.2 billion dollars in a single day, the biggest one-day loss since at least 2019. When a stock jumps 33 percent at the open, anyone who bet against it has to buy it back, and that buying pushes it higher, and that forces more buying.”

    Leo — the risk case

    Risk and trust

    “There’s a trap here, and it’s thinking that Monday fixed everything. First, the stock is still down. Even after a 31.48 percent day, it’s still about 5 percent below the $173.73 close on 11 November, and still about 32 percent below the October high of $243.37. Monday was a recovery, not a full repair.”

    “The regulatory cloud hasn’t fully cleared. That 8-K answers the national security question, and that’s important. But the SEC probe into Nvidia’s accounting is still open, as far as we know. The California subpoena is still there. Huang still has that closed-door House committee interview on December the 1st. And it’s the same board — the same people who pushed him out on the 12th, ruled out his return, and then reversed themselves in a weekend. What does that say about governance?”

    Where Maya and Leo land

    Leadership restoration was the trigger, short covering and a market-wide bounce were the fuel, and the earnings gave buyers the confidence to come back in size. But the job isn’t done — the SEC probe, the December 1st hearing, and the board’s own credibility are still open questions. What to watch next: not just whether the stock holds these gains, but what comes out of that hearing, and whether the board finally tells a proper succession story.

    Maya: Good morning, and welcome back to Wins Finance Radio. I'm Maya.

    Leo: And I'm Leo. And Maya, what a difference a weekend makes.

    Maya: Honestly. On Friday we were sitting here talking about Nvidia down 14 percent, at 125 dollars 53. This morning it's $165.05, up 31.48 percent in a single day. And the volume — around 776 million shares. The most-traded stock in America again, but this time, everyone was buying.

    Leo: Right, and that's the question for the next seven minutes. Why Nvidia, why that much volume, and why Monday? Because on Friday, this stock looked finished.

    Maya: And the answer, Leo, is pretty simple this time. He's back. Jensen Huang is back. On Sunday, the board voted unanimously to reinstate him as president, CEO and director, effective immediately. They announced it before the market opened on Monday, and the stock just took off. It opened at 167 dollars 04, up about 33 percent, and at one point it was up near 171 dollars 50.

    Leo: And can we just sit with how extraordinary that reversal is? Six days earlier, this same board had ruled out his return. And then, over the weekend, they did a complete U-turn.

    Maya: What changed their minds, do you think?

    Leo: A few things, all at once. President Trump had been pressing publicly for Huang's return. A reinstatement petition had gathered more than 180,000 signatures, Elon Musk's among them. And Nvidia filed an 8-K on Monday saying the board had consulted senior government officials and determined that Huang posed no national security risk. That was the cloud hanging over the whole thing, the China question, and the board just cleared it.

    Leo: Pretty much. And the market's verdict was instant. About a trillion dollars of market value added back in a matter of minutes. Analysts piled in too — Melius, Bernstein, StoneX, Oppenheimer, they all upgraded the stock on Monday.

    Maya: Okay, but can I add a second reason, because I don't think the board vote alone explains 776 million shares. That's short covering, Leo. That's a squeeze.

    Leo: Oh, absolutely. The bears got crushed on Monday.

    Maya: They really did. ORTEX estimated short sellers lost about 12.2 billion dollars in a single day, the biggest one-day loss since at least 2019. When a stock jumps 33 percent at the open, anyone who bet against it has to buy it back, and that buying pushes it higher, and that forces more buying.

    Leo: And reason number three, the market itself. This wasn't just a Nvidia day, it was a everything day. The Nasdaq was up 6.51 percent to 24,090, the S&P 500 up 4.73 percent to 7,237, and the Dow up 3.01 percent to 49,595. After the worst week in years, the whole market wanted a reason to bounce, and Nvidia handed them one.

    Maya: So we've got three forces now — the CEO coming back, the short squeeze, and a broad market rebound. On Friday, 633 million shares traded because everyone wanted out at the same time. On Monday, 776 million traded because everyone wanted back in at the same time, plus the shorts being dragged in.

    Leo: I like that. But Maya, can I play the sceptic for a minute? Because I think there's a trap here, and it's thinking that Monday fixed everything. First, the stock is still down. Even after a 31.48 percent day, it's still about 5 percent below the $173.73 close on 11 November, and still about 32 percent below the October high of $243.37. Monday was a recovery, not a full repair.

    Maya: Fair. But look at it from the other side, Leo. The business never actually changed. Those earnings we talked about on Friday — record revenue, 115.8 billion dollars, up 103 percent, guidance of 130 billion — none of that went away. At Friday's close, Nvidia was trading at about 13 times forward earnings, against a five-year average of about 41. Even now, after the jump, it's only about 17 times. The market wasn't pricing the business on Friday, it was pricing the fear. And the fear was about one man leaving. Now he's back.

    Leo: I hear you, and the valuation point is strong. But here's my second worry — the regulatory cloud hasn't fully cleared. That 8-K answers the national security question, and that's important. But the SEC probe into Nvidia's accounting is still open, as far as we know. The California subpoena is still there. Huang still has that closed-door House committee interview on December the 1st. None of that disappeared on Monday.

    Maya: That's true, and I won't pretend otherwise. But do you remember what Steve Sosnick at Interactive Brokers said to Reuters? That Jensen Huang was, quote, too big to be fired. And I think Monday proved him right. The customers, the roadmap, the whole AI story — it runs through him.

    Leo: They learned that the expensive way. And that's actually my third worry, Maya. It's the same board. The same people who pushed him out on the 12th, ruled out his return, and then reversed themselves in a weekend — they're still running the company. What does that say about governance? Investors should be asking that, even while they're celebrating.

    Maya: Okay, that's a fair challenge. So where does that leave us? I think we're actually closer than it sounds.

    Leo: I think we are. The rebound was real, and it was mostly about leadership. The board bringing Huang back removed the single biggest reason for the sell-off, and the market repriced that in minutes. But the size of the move — 31.48 percent, 776 million shares — that was the squeeze and the broad rebound piling on top. It wasn't all thoughtful buying.

    Maya: And I'd add — the volume tells you this wasn't indifference, just like Friday. On Friday, the volume said everyone had lost conviction at once. On Monday, it said conviction came flooding back, plus a lot of forced buying. And the fundamentals give that conviction something to stand on. The earnings are real, and at these levels the stock is still cheap against its own history.

    Leo: Agreed. So the consensus is: leadership restoration was the trigger, short covering and a market-wide bounce were the fuel, and the earnings gave buyers the confidence to come back in size. But the job isn't done. The SEC probe, the December 1st hearing, and the board's own credibility — those are still open questions.

    Maya: And for anyone listening, that's what to watch next. Not just whether the stock holds these gains, but what comes out of that hearing, and whether the board finally tells us a proper succession story.

    Leo: And if they do, then Monday might be remembered as the day the AI trade found its footing again. If they don't, then it was just a very expensive relief rally.

    Maya: Thanks for listening. I'm Maya.

    Leo: And I'm Leo. We'll be back tomorrow morning with a fresh briefing on Wins Finance Radio.

    Maya: Take care.
