    Discussions about Nvidia

    Nvidia (NVDA) — the most-traded US stock on Tuesday 24 November 2026, closed at $155.38, down $9.67 (−5.86%), on about 379 million shares, the day after Monday’s 31.48% rebound to $165.05. Maya and Leo work through the slide from opposite sides.

    Maya — the opportunity case

    Fundamentals and opportunity

    Maya argues the pullback is a healthy breather after a 31% one-day spike, not the start of another collapse. Even after Tuesday’s fall, Nvidia is still up about 24% from Friday’s close of $125.53, and the business hasn’t changed since Monday: record revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103%, earnings per share of $2.68, and guidance of around $133 billion for the current quarter.

    The customers still need the chips, the numbers are still extraordinary, and at $155 the stock is still a long way below its October high of $243.37. Monday proved the stock can move 30% on a headline; Tuesday proved it can give back 6% just as fast — that kind of volatility isn’t a buying signal by itself, it’s a warning to be patient.

    Leo — the risk case

    Risk and trust

    Leo argues Tuesday was the hangover after Monday’s stampede: a 31% day doesn’t happen because investors calmly re-rated a company — it happened because the CEO came back, the shorts got squeezed, and the whole market bounced at once. On Tuesday the squeeze was over and buyers had to decide whether they actually wanted to own the stock at $165.

    The sell-off was chip-wide (Micron down more than 10%, AMD about 9%, Broadcom 3%), Anthropic fell below its IPO price, and good results were sold anyway (Analog Devices -17.4%, HPE -16.3%), which says the doubts are about the whole AI trade and whether the big tech companies are making their money back on AI spending. The SEC probe into Nvidia’s accounting is still open and Jensen Huang is still summoned before House committees on 1 December, so every wobble will be read through that lens until then.

    Where Maya and Leo land

    Monday was relief plus a squeeze; Tuesday was the market asking for proof. In the near term, expect more volatility until the PCE inflation data on Wednesday and the 1 December hearing. Longer term, the demand story is intact, but the stock has to earn the re-rating — it can't just be handed one in a single day. What to watch next: PCE inflation data on Wednesday, Dell and HP reporting after the close, markets closed Thursday for Thanksgiving with an early close on Friday, and then 1 December.
